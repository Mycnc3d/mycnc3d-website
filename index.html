Rethinking FDM

Beyond conventional layer-by-layer manufacturing.

Conventional FDM is built around a simple principle: material is deposited in successive planar layers. This approach is highly versatile, but it also introduces fundamental constraints.

Part orientation, anisotropic mechanical properties, overhangs, deposition direction and toolpath geometry are strongly influenced by the planar nature of the process. At higher deposition rates, extrusion performance, thermal behaviour and machine dynamics introduce additional limitations.

MYCNC3D investigates these constraints as parts of the same manufacturing problem. Rather than optimizing a single component in isolation, our research considers the interaction between machine motion, material deposition and toolpath generation.

Engineering approach

Engineering through experimentation.

Advanced manufacturing technologies cannot be developed from simulation or theory alone.

MYCNC3D follows an iterative engineering methodology in which ideas are transformed into prototypes, instrumented, tested and progressively improved. Failures and limitations are treated as engineering data.

01Design
02Prototype
03Instrument
04Test
05Analyse
06Iterate

Depending on the research subject, experiments may involve machine prototypes, extrusion test systems, sensors, manufacturing trials, G-code analysis, software experiments and toolpath validation.
