Project overview

Layer orientation becomes a manufacturing variable.

Physical non-planar printing has been demonstrated on representative FDM hardware.

MYCNC3D is investigating deposition strategies in which layer orientation becomes a manufacturing variable rather than a fixed global constraint. The research focuses on local deposition orientation, inclined and curved trajectories, overhang capability, surface continuity and the physical clearance limits imposed by the nozzle and toolhead geometry.

A mathematically valid trajectory is not necessarily manufacturable. For this reason, generated paths are evaluated through physical printing experiments to determine practical limits related to slope, curvature, extrusion stability and machine clearance.

Current experimental work focuses on extending successful demonstrations into repeatable and reusable manufacturing strategies.

Why it matters

A valid trajectory must also be physically manufacturable.

A mathematically valid non-planar path can still be impossible to manufacture because of toolhead interference, insufficient nozzle clearance, excessive local slope or unsuitable extrusion conditions.

The research therefore combines geometry with manufacturability.

Current focus

What we are investigating now.

Local layer orientation

Changing deposition direction according to part geometry rather than using one global build direction.

Overhang strategies

Evaluating how inclined or curved deposition paths alter support requirements and accessible geometry.

Surface continuity

Studying continuous paths that follow a surface rather than a stepped planar approximation.

Nozzle clearance

Determining practical limits imposed by nozzle, heater block and toolhead geometry.

Slope & curvature limits

Identifying path geometries that remain printable with stable material deposition.

Physical validation

Comparing generated trajectories with real printed results.

Engineering approach

Generated paths are only accepted after physical testing.

The research cycle combines geometry generation, path inspection, machine preparation and controlled printing tests. Results are used to refine clearance rules, slope limits and deposition strategies.

Current status

EXPERIMENTAL

Physical non-planar printing has been demonstrated. The current phase is focused on converting successful physical demonstrations into repeatable and reusable manufacturing rules.

Next milestone

From successful demonstrations to reusable manufacturing rules.

The next milestone is to establish repeatable path-generation and machine-clearance rules that can be applied across multiple geometries rather than individual test parts.

Progress toward UNDER DEVELOPMENT will require defined limits for slope, curvature and tool clearance, together with reduced manual correction and repeatable physical results.
