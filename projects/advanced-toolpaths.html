Project overview

The toolpath becomes an engineering object.

Multiple families of generated toolpaths have been physically printed and evaluated.

MYCNC3D develops experimental methods for generating additive-manufacturing trajectories directly from geometric and process parameters. Research includes parametric path generation, variable deposition geometry, non-planar trajectories, path ordering, motion continuity, machine-specific post-processing and flow-aware speed control.

A geometrically correct path is not automatically a reliable manufacturing path. Extrusion demand, acceleration, intersections, local bead geometry, continuity and machine constraints must all be considered during trajectory generation.

Current research focuses on converting successful geometry-specific methods into reusable and parameterised trajectory-generation components.

Why it matters

Complex motion requires process-aware trajectory generation.

A geometrically valid path is not automatically a good manufacturing path. Extrusion throughput, acceleration, local bead geometry, intersections, continuity and machine kinematics all affect whether the motion can be executed reliably.

Toolpath generation must therefore connect geometry with physical process limits.

Current focus

What we are investigating now.

Parametric path generation

Reusable path families from explicit geometric and process parameters.

Variable deposition geometry

Local control of bead width, height or path shape.

Flow-aware speed control

Adapting machine speed to local extrusion demand and volumetric-flow limits.

Non-planar trajectories

Generating curved or inclined paths beyond planar layer assumptions.

Path ordering & continuity

Reducing unnecessary travel, intersections and discontinuities.

Machine-specific post-processing

Converting generic manufacturing intent into commands compatible with target kinematics and controls.

Engineering approach

Build the path, print it, analyse the result.

Experimental trajectories are generated, inspected and physically validated whenever possible. The printed result is used to refine geometric rules, process parameters and path sequencing.

Current status

EXPERIMENTAL

Multiple families of generated toolpaths have been physically printed and evaluated. The current objective is to convert successful geometry-specific methods into reusable trajectory-generation components.

Next milestone

From geometry-specific scripts to a reusable toolpath engine.

The next milestone is to generalise trajectory generation across families of geometries while systematically accounting for extrusion flow, speed, acceleration, path continuity, intersections and machine constraints.

Progress toward UNDER DEVELOPMENT will be reached when the same generation logic can be parameterised and reused without being rewritten for each individual test geometry.
